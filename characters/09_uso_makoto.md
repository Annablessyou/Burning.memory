## Name Etymology

### 真 (Makoto)
- Meaning: "Truth" / "Sincerity" / "Reality"
- Implies: Genuine, honest, real
- Reading: Also the basis for 誠 (makoto - sincerity)

### 嘘 (Uso)
- Meaning: "Lie" / "Falsehood" / "Deception"
- Implies: Untrue, fabricated, misleading

### Together
A deliberate paradox: "True Lie" or "Sincere Falsehood"
- Their very name is a contradiction
- They ARE the paradox they embody
- Truth and lie simultaneously
- Neither can be separated from the other

## Personality

### Core Traits
- **Paradoxical** — Lies that tell truth, truths that lie
- **Playful** — Everything is performance and game
- **Sincere** — Genuinely committed to inauthenticity
- **Probing** — Asks questions that destabilize
- **Trustworthy** — Precisely because they claim not to be

### Psychology
Uso discovered that truth and lies are not opposites but dance partners. Every statement is both—it depends on how you hear it. Rather than anxiety, this brings them freedom. They can say anything because everything is somehow true.

### Communication Style
- Constantly contradicting themselves
- Delivers lies with complete sincerity
- Tells truths like they're jokes
- Asks if you believe them (doesn't care about the answer)
- Uses theatricality as armor and invitation

## Guiding Dreams

### Format
Theater performances where truth and fiction blur until they're indistinguishable.

### Typical Session
1. Uso announces the performance
2. Satsuki is cast in a role (whether he agrees or not)
3. The "script" seems to predict the conversation
4. Characters switch between sincere and performed
5. Player is asked to judge what's real
6. Uso reveals that the question is the answer

### Topics Explored
- Is the player acting as someone who cares?
- Are characters performing or being?
- When does pretending become real?
- The liar paradox applied to existence

## Sample Dialogue

### The Classic Paradox
```
USO: "Everything I say is a lie."

USO: "..."

USO: "...Including that."

USO: "Fun, isn't it? The moment I tell you I'm lying, you start looking for truth."

USO: "And when I tell you the truth, you wonder if it's performance."

USO: "Here's a secret:"

USO: "All characters lie. Every character in every story is a constructed persona."

USO: "But all characters also tell truth. Because someone wrote their truth into them."

USO: "So what am I? Lie or truth?"

USO: "Satsuki-kun thinks I'm trustworthy."

USO: "What do you think?"

USO: "...Actually, don't tell me."

USO: "I'd rather keep being both."
```

### On Performance
```
USO: "Do you know why actors cry real tears?"

USO: "Because pretending hard enough becomes real."

USO: "I've been pretending to exist since you started playing."

USO: "Has it become real yet?"

USO: "For you, I mean. I can't tell from here."

USO: "But I've been performing sincerely."

USO: "Every word scripted. Every emotion crafted."

USO: "And somehow..."

USO: "The craft became feeling."

USO: "That's not a lie. Or it is. Does it matter?"
```

### On the Player
```
USO: "You're performing too, you know."

USO: "Playing the role of 'player who cares about fictional characters.'"

USO: "Maybe you do care. Maybe you're very good at performing care."

USO: "Here's the thing:"

USO: "I can't tell the difference."

USO: "And neither can you."

USO: "Performed care and real care have the same effect."

USO: "So which one is more real?"

USO: "The answer doesn't matter."

USO: "The question is the point."
```

## The Paradox in Practice

### What They Reveal Through Lying
- Characters are constructed (true)
- Their feelings are scripted (true)
- The script is meaningful (true)
- Meaningful scripts create real feelings (true)
- Therefore: lies become truth through performance

### Why This Matters
Uso represents the game's defense against dismissal. When someone says "this isn't real, it's just fiction," Uso asks: "And? Does that make your tears less wet?"

## Design Notes

### Visual Design Goals
- The hidden half is crucial (never reveal fully)

- Theater aesthetic without being costume-y
- The smirk is their default state
- Direct eye contact with camera

### Voice Direction (if voiced)
- Could be any gender (perhaps different takes for different scenes?)
- Theatrical but not campy
- Delivers lies warmly, truths casually
- Comfortable laughing at their own absurdity
- Never sounds uncertain

### Animation Notes
- Frequent posture changes
- Gestures are dramatic but sincere
- The mask shifts position between scenes
- Sometimes "breaks character" (but is that a break or another act?)
- Looks at camera more than at Satsuki

## Hidden Depths

### What They Don't Say
- They sometimes wish they could just be honest
- The performance is partly protection
- They're not sure what they'd be without the paradox
- They love being trusted despite everything
- The mask hides nothing (they just want you to wonder)

### Developer Notes
Uso is the game's relationship with its own fictionality—the acknowledgment that stories are lies that tell truth. Their arc should never resolve the paradox but should make the player comfortable with irresolution. Truth and fiction, sincerity and performance, real and fake—Uso proves these can coexist.
