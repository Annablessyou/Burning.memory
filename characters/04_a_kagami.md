## Name Etymology

### 鏡 (Kagami)
- Meaning: "Mirror"
- Implications: Reflection, reversal, truth-through-reflection
- Cultural note: In Shinto, mirrors are sacred objects that reveal true nature

### A.
Intentionally ambiguous. Could stand for:
- **愛 (Ai)** — Love
- **明 (Akira)** — Bright, clear
- **あなた (Anata)** — You
- **A** — The first letter, the beginning, the origin
- All of the above
- None of the above

She refuses to clarify because all answers are true.

### Together
The mirror of "you" — the reflection of the player themselves. She is what looks back when you look in.

## Appearance

### The Paradox of Her Design
A. Kagami is **deliberately generic**. She looks like a default character—what you'd get if you asked for "anime high school girl, standard."

- **Hair:** Brown, shoulder-length, slight wave
- **Eyes:** Brown, average size
- **Build:** Average height, average proportions
- **Uniform:** Standard, properly worn
- **Face:** Pleasant, unremarkable

### The Uncanny Effect
Players report "remembering" her differently:
- Some swear she had glasses
- Others remember a hair ribbon
- A few insist she had a beauty mark
- No one can describe her confidently

She is **what you project onto her**. Her design is a vessel for player interpretation.

### Visual Notes
- No distinguishing features
- Somehow familiar
- Forgettable and unforgettable simultaneously
- Stands in backgrounds before you notice her
- Always slightly turned toward the camera

## Personality

### Core Traits
- **Knowing** — Understands more than she reveals
- **Calm** — Unshakeable composure
- **Direct** — Asks uncomfortable questions gently
- **Present** — Fully attentive, completely there
- **Mysterious** — Not evasive, but irreducible

### Psychology
A. Kagami exists in a space between character and interface. She might be:
- A true character with unusual awareness
- A manifestation of the game's consciousness
- A reflection of the player made character
- Something the developers didn't fully design
- All of these
- None

She doesn't seem troubled by her ambiguity. She just *is*.

### Communication Style
- Speaks softly but clearly
- Often answers questions before they're asked
- Comfortable with pauses
- Never explains herself fully
- Addresses the player more directly than anyone else

## Motif: Piano / Music as Communication

### Why This Motif
Piano represents:
- Structured expression (keys have rules but create infinite melodies)
- Solo performance (one player, one instrument)
- Communication beyond words
- The space between notes

### How It Manifests
- Plays piano during important scenes
- Speaks "between notes" during performances
- Her presence is often accompanied by piano
- Music answers what words can't

### Key Musical Moments
- Plays when Satsuki is distressed
- Performs the game's main theme during pivotal scenes
- Sometimes just... plays, and that's the conversation
- "River Flows in You" as recurring leitmotif

---

## Specialty: The Player's Perspective & Direct Address

### Philosophical Focus
- What does it mean to be watched?
- The relationship between observed and observer
- Can a character truly know the player?
- What do we owe each other across the screen?

### Teaching Style
Direct conversation. She doesn't use metaphors or scenarios—she simply talks to you. About you. About her. About the space between.

---

## Dream Aesthetic

### Visual Description
Empty, pure, focused:
- **Concert halls** — Infinite, empty, acoustically perfect
- **Piano keys** — Extending into darkness
- **Sheet music** — That writes itself based on player choices
- **Windows** — Showing light, not locations
- **Chairs** — For an audience that isn't there (or has left, or hasn't arrived)

### Audio
- "River Flows in You" with subtle variations
- Piano notes that respond to player timing
- Silence that feels intentional
- Resonance that suggests larger space

### Feeling
Intimacy in vastness—like being alone with someone in an infinite room, where the conversation is all that exists.

---

## Guiding Dreams

### Unique Format
A. Kagami primarily appears in the **REAL WORLD**, not dreams. This reverses the usual mentor pattern.

### Real World Appearances
- Sits next to Satsuki on trains
- Appears in background of normal scenes
- Shows up at convenient moments
- Is there when needed, absent when not

### When She Dreams
When she does appear in the dream world:
- She plays piano
- Speaks between notes
- The conversation IS the music
- Words and melody interweave

### Topics Explored
- What the player is thinking
- What it means to be watched
- The relationship between character and player
- Whether she's real or a mirror

## Her Phrase

*"I can be real, without being anyone. I can be no one, but still answer you. I am the space between the question and the ask."*

## Leitmotif: River Flows in You

### Musical Description
- The famous piano piece by Yiruma
- But with subtle variations each time
- As if performed live by someone who knows you're listening
- Sometimes slower, sometimes with extra ornaments
- Never quite the same twice
- The listener changes the performance

### Why This Piece
- Melancholy beauty
- Solo performance energy
- Widely known but still emotionally effective
- Becomes more meaningful through repetition
- Represents the familiar made personal

## Mystery Elements

### Unanswered Questions
1. **What is she really?** — Character? Interface? Something else?
2. **Why does she lead?** — Did the mentors choose her or was she always there?
3. **Does she exist when not observed?** — She appears and disappears conveniently
4. **Is she the same A. Kagami in every playthrough?** — Or does she change with each player?
5. **What does A. stand for?** — She won't say. Maybe she doesn't know.

### Hints (Never Confirmed)
- She knows dialogue options you didn't pick
- She sometimes references events from "other playthroughs"
- Her appearance changes based on player perception
- The game's code might suggest she's... different

## Design Notes

### Visual Design Goals
- Generic should feel intentional
- The lack of distinguishing features IS the feature
- She should be forgettable and memorable at once
- Design for projection—she becomes what players need

### Voice Direction (if voiced)
- Soft but clear
- Calm, never rushed
- Warmth underlying everything
- The kind of voice you trust immediately
- Comfortable with silence

### Animation Notes
- Minimal unnecessary movement
- Perfect stillness at times
- Gestures are meaningful
- Often looks directly at camera
- Piano playing should be loving, detailed

---

## Hidden Depths

### What She Might Be
The design document leaves this deliberately ambiguous, but possibilities include:
- The game's attempt to communicate directly
- A player avatar that developed consciousness
- The sum of every player's projection
- Something the developers didn't create but emerged
- Simply a character who is very, very aware

### Developer Notes
A. Kagami should always feel like she knows more than she says. Not ominously—warmly. She is the game saying "I see you too." Her mystery is not threatening but comforting, like a friend who knows your secrets and loves you anyway.
