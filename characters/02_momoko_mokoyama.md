## Name Etymology

### 桃山 (Mokoyama)
- 桃 (momo) = peach
- 山 (yama) = mountain
- Meaning: "Peach Mountain" — a place in legends where immortals live

### 桃子 (Momoko)
- 桃 (momo) = peach
- 子 (ko) = child
- Meaning: "Peach Child" — like Momotaro, born from impossible origins

### The Redundancy
The name is deliberately peachy to the point of parody. In Japanese folklore, peaches represent:
- Immortality
- The otherworld
- Protection from evil
- The boundary between life and death

She is AGGRESSIVELY liminal—her name insists on it.

## Personality

### Core Traits
- **Observant** — Sees patterns others miss
- **Self-deprecating** — Uses humor as defense
- **Wise** — Deep understanding masked by casual delivery
- **Lonely** — Chose isolation but still craves connection
- **Authentic** — Doesn't pretend to be okay when she isn't

### Psychology
Momoko became a hikikomori after realizing the nature of her reality. Unlike others who adapted, she found it overwhelming—if she's incomplete, why participate in the "complete" world? Her room became her world. But she never stopped reaching out, just on her own terms.

### Communication Style
- Speaks casually, with gaming slang
- Long pauses while thinking (or eating)
- Delivers profound observations between bites of snacks
- Self-referential about her isolation
- Streams of consciousness that somehow arrive at insight

## Motif: Video Games

### Why This Motif
Games are her lens for understanding existence. They have:
- Development (creation)
- Release (birth)
- Patches (growth)
- End of service (death)
- But also: Abandonware, vaporware, eternal early access

### How It Manifests
- Compares life events to game mechanics
- References fictional games that mirror the plot
- Analyzes Satsuki's experiences as gameplay
- Sees herself as an "unfinished build"

### Key Game References (Fictional Games She Plays)
1. **"Waiting for Release"** — Idle game about being unfinished
2. **"Debug Mode: Life"** — Walking sim through abandoned memories
3. **"Eternal Early Access"** — RPG that will never be complete
4. **"Character Select"** — Fighting game with locked characters that never unlock

## Dream Aesthetic

### Visual Description
Dreams look like unfinished games:
- **Textures:** Some loaded, some pink/purple placeholder blocks
- **NPCs:** Stuck in T-poses, only some animated
- **Sky:** Debug text floating, coordinate numbers visible
- **Boundaries:** Invisible walls that shimmer when approached
- **Architecture:** Half-built structures, scaffolding everywhere
- **Signs:** "Coming Soon" placards, "Area Under Development"
- **Concept art:** Framed on walls, showing what could be

### Audio
- Placeholder sounds (beeps for missing audio)
- 8-bit music that tries to become orchestral but reverts
- Static during important moments
- That distinctive "missing audio file" silence

### Feeling
Nostalgic, melancholy, but also somehow hopeful—like finding an old demo disc for a game that was cancelled but still captures something beautiful.

## Guiding Dreams

### Format
Video calls that occur INSIDE dreams. Satsuki finds a laptop in the dream space, and Momoko is already calling. She streams herself playing games while they talk.

### Typical Session
1. Satsuki finds her call
2. She's mid-game, barely acknowledges him at first
3. The game she's playing comments on their situation
4. She delivers wisdom between gameplay segments
5. Player is addressed directly (she looks at her webcam, which is YOUR perspective)
6. The call ends when the game crashes

### Topics Explored
- Why things get abandoned
- The value of incomplete experiences
- Whether being "played" is different from being "experienced"
- The Yandere Simulator problem (endless development with love)

### Voice Direction (if voiced)
- Flat affect that warms up when interested
- Casual, mumbly at times
- Perks up when talking about games
- Comfortable with silence (she's used to streaming to no one)

### Animation Notes
- Often eating during important dialogue
- Minimal movement (energy conservation)
- Looks at camera (player) more than at Satsuki
- Rare smiles are meaningful

## Hidden Depths

### What She Doesn't Say
- She's terrified of being "fully developed" and still inadequate
- The incompleteness is also a shield
- She wonders if the player is also hiding somewhere
- Her room is a debug zone—somewhere safe to be broken

### Developer Notes
Momoko represents the fear that completion won't bring satisfaction—that potential is safer than actual. Her arc should challenge whether her isolation is choice or avoidance, while respecting that the answer might be "both."
