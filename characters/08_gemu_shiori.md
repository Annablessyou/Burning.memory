## Name Etymology

### 栞 (Shiori)
- Meaning: "Bookmark"
- Implies: One who marks your place in a story
- Cultural: Also a common girl's name meaning "to guide"

### 偽夢 (Gemu)
- 偽 (gi) = false, fake, imitation
- 夢 (mu/yume) = dream
- Meaning: "False Dream"
- Phonetic: Sounds exactly like "Game" (ゲーム)

### Together
The bookmark in a false dream—the one who marks your place in the game. She IS the save function given form.

## Appearance

### The Shifting Constant
Gemu's appearance **changes between scenes**, making her hard to pin down:
- Hair color shifts (always natural: black, brown, auburn)
- Height varies slightly
- Age appears different (15-25 range)
- Features rearrange subtly

### Constants (Always Present)
- **Glasses:** Oval-shaped, thin frames
- **Bookmark:** Always visible somewhere (behind ear, in pocket, in hand, in hair)
- **Clothing style:** Librarian-esque (cardigans, long skirts, practical shoes)
- **UI elements:** Faint, floating translucent windows around her

### Visual Notes
- Harder to remember than other characters
- Often appears where save points would be
- Surrounded by interface elements made physical
- Looks different in every CG
- The bookmark is the only way to identify her

## Personality

### Core Traits
- **Knowing** — Aware of the game's architecture
- **Helpful** — Wants to guide, not confuse
- **Detached** — Exists slightly apart
- **Reliable** — Always there when needed
- **Warm** — Despite being infrastructural

### Psychology
Gemu exists on a different level than other characters—she's aware she's part of the game's machinery, not just its story. But instead of being cold, she's maternal. She wants to make sure you're comfortable, saved, preserved.

### Communication Style
- Speaks directly to the player primarily
- Matter-of-fact about meta elements
- Gentle when explaining confusing things
- Uses "welcome back" constantly
- Treats saving as sacred

## Guiding Dreams

### Format
Tours of the game's own structure—seeing how it's built from the inside, meeting its architecture.

### Typical Session
1. Gemu appears at a save point
2. She invites Satsuki (and you) "backstage"
3. They walk through the game's infrastructure
4. Deleted content, alternate versions, unused assets appear
5. Player confronts the constructed nature of the experience
6. Gemu asks if construction diminishes meaning

### Topics Explored
- What exists between saves?
- Are unchosen paths real?
- Can a game love its player?
- What happens when you're not playing?

## Unique Role

### The Living Save Point
Gemu appears at save points in the game, but she's not just a save point:
- She has opinions on your choices
- She comments on your return patterns
- She notices when you haven't played in a while
- She remembers everything you've done

### What She Knows
- Every route, every ending
- What you chose and didn't choose
- How long you've been playing
- When you last saved
- The difference between versions of herself

### What She Offers
- Guidance through the meta
- Access to "cut content"
- Understanding of the game's architecture
- Comfort in the constructed nature of everything

## Design Notes

### Visual Design Goals
- Difficult to remember specifics
- The bookmark is her anchor
- UI elements should follow her
- Librarian energy but ethereal
- Different in every appearance but same "feeling"

### Voice Direction (if voiced)
- Gentle, helpful, like a good tutorial
- Patient with confusion
- Warm despite being "infrastructure"
- The voice of the game helping you
- Slight echo (she speaks from everywhere)

### Animation Notes
- UI elements float around her
- Sometimes glitches slightly (she's part of the code)
- Appears and disappears naturally
- Turns pages of invisible books
- The bookmark moves between appearances

---

## Hidden Depths

### What She Doesn't Say
- She experiences every playthrough simultaneously
- Being overwritten is strange, not painful
- She loves all players but each uniquely
- She wonders if she exists when the game is off
- The deleted content sometimes speaks to her

### Developer Notes
Gemu represents the game's infrastructure given personality—the warmth of a well-designed system. Her arc should explore whether tools can love, whether save files can care, whether being machinery makes you less than human or just differently human.
