## Name Etymology

### 向川 (Mukougawa)
- 向 (mukou) = facing, opposite, beyond
- 川 (kawa/gawa) = river
- Meaning: "Opposite Bank" / "The Other Side of the River"
- Implies: A place you can see but not easily reach, separation with visibility

### ミラ (Mira)
- Foreign name (written in katakana, not Japanese origin)
- Multiple meanings:
  - Latin: "wonder" / "admirable"
  - Sounds like "mirror" (ミラー)
  - Mira: a famous variable star (brightest then fading)
- Implies: Foreign, wonderful, reflective, changing

### Together
"The wonder on the opposite bank" — something beautiful across an unbridgeable distance that somehow reflects you back.

## The Travel Journal

### Significance
She carries a worn travel journal:
- Stamps from places that don't exist
- Stickers from fictional destinations
- Notes in multiple languages (some invented)
- Maps of emotional rather than physical terrain
- Evidence of a journey that can't be traced

### What It Contains
- Observations about this world (from outsider view)

## Personality

### Core Traits
- **Observant** — Sees this world with fresh eyes
- **Empathetic** — Understands distance and longing
- **Curious** — Fascinated by what's "normal" here
- **Lonely** — Perpetual outsider, by nature and circumstance
- **Hopeful** — Believes bridges can be built

### Psychology
Mira is always foreign—never quite home anywhere. Rather than tragedy, she's found this liberating. She understands the player's position better than any other character because she's also observing from outside.

### Communication Style
- Speaks Japanese with occasional "foreign" syntax
- Phrases things differently (correct but unusual)
- Asks questions natives wouldn't think to ask
- Comfortable with linguistic gaps
- Sometimes uses words from other languages

## Guiding Dreams

### Format
Crossing borders between dream worlds, translating the untranslatable, finding connection across gaps.

### Typical Session
1. Mira meets Satsuki at a border
2. They cross into another mentor's domain together
3. She translates between worlds
4. Cultural differences become philosophical ones
5. Player is acknowledged as the ultimate foreigner
6. The gap between worlds becomes a place itself

### Topics Explored
- Can the player ever truly "enter" this world?
- What is lost and gained in translation?
- Is understanding possible across fundamental difference?
- The gift of the outside perspective

## Hidden Depths

### What She Doesn't Say
- She misses a home she can't remember
- The perpetual travel is partly running
- She hopes the player knows what "home" feels like
- She wants to belong but has forgotten how
- This game is the closest thing to home

### Developer Notes
Mira represents the player's position in the game—always outside, always observing, never fully part of. Her arc should validate that position while showing it has its own value. Being between is not failure to belong; it's a different kind of belonging.
