## Name Etymology

### 火花 (Hibana)
- 火 (hi) = fire
- 花 (hana/bana) = flower
- Meaning: "Spark" or "Firework" — literally "fire flower"
- Implies: Brief, brilliant, searching in the dark

### ペコ (Peko)
- Mimetic word from "peko peko" (hungry stomach sounds)
- Also evokes "pecking" — like a bird searching for crumbs
- Implies: Hungry for meaning, picks at details others miss
- Together: A spark that hungers, searching for evidence in darkness

## Personality

### Core Traits
- **Observant** — Notices everything, processes later
- **Methodical** — Everything gets documented
- **Passionate** — Intense about finding truth
- **Intuitive** — Leaps of logic that turn out correct
- **Protective** — Collects evidence partly to understand, partly to defend

### Psychology
Peko deals with the absurdity of existence by insisting it leaves evidence. If she can find proof—of anything—then things become real through documentation. Her scavenging is part coping mechanism, part genuine detective work.

### Communication Style
- Short sentences, declarative
- Shows more than tells
- Dramatic pauses before revelations
- Uses detective terminology unironically
- Often trails off when thinking, then returns mid-thought

## Guiding Dreams

### Format
Scavenger hunts through meaning itself. The player must find connections Peko has left, while she provides commentary and reveals deeper layers.

### Typical Session
1. Satsuki arrives to find evidence scattered
2. Peko briefs him (and you) on the "case"
3. Player navigates the space finding clues
4. Peko interprets findings, reveals connections
5. The "solution" raises more questions than it answers
6. She collects the evidence for later

### Topics Explored
- Can you prove the player exists?
- What evidence do dreams leave behind?
- Is interpretation discovery or invention?
- The difference between footprint and foot

## The Real World Role

### Unique Function
Peko is the only mentor who leaves clues in the WAKING world for the player. While other mentors primarily exist in dreams, Peko's investigation crosses boundaries.

### How This Works
- Strange items appear in Satsuki's apartment
- Notes in his textbooks that weren't there before
- Rearranged objects that form patterns
- Evidence boards in their shared space
- The player must interpret what she leaves

### Why This Matters
She's building a bridge between worlds. Her evidence is proof that the boundary can be crossed—that things can reach from inside the game to the player's attention.

## Hidden Depths

### What She Doesn't Say
- She's afraid of what she might prove
- Some evidence suggests conclusions she doesn't want
- What if the case solves itself badly?
- What if proving you exist proves she doesn't?

### Developer Notes
Peko represents the drive to understand even the incomprehensible—the need to impose meaning on chaos. Her arc should explore whether evidence creates truth or just the illusion of it.
