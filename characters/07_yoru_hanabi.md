## Name Etymology

### 花火 (Hanabi)
- 花 (hana) = flower
- 火 (hi/bi) = fire
- Meaning: "Fireworks" — literally "flower fire"
- Implies: Beautiful, brief, celebrated, memorable

### 夜留 (Yoru)
- 夜 (yoru) = night
- 留 (ru/tome) = to stay, to remain
- Meaning: "Night Stays" or "Remaining Night"
- Implies: What lingers after darkness, the afterimage

### Together
Fireworks that stay in the night—the afterimage of something bright that should have faded but didn't. She captures what should be fleeting.

### Relation to Peko
Her name deliberately echoes Peko Hibana (火花):
- Hibana = Spark (fire-flower)
- Hanabi = Fireworks (flower-fire)
- They are conceptual mirrors—Peko seeks evidence of now, Yoru captures evidence of when

## The Camera

### Significance
A vintage film camera (not digital) that she carries everywhere:
- Can't see pictures immediately (must develop)
- Each shot is precious (limited film)
- The delay between capture and viewing is intentional
- Represents memory's gap between experience and recollection

### What She Photographs
- Moments that feel significant
- The player's "presence" (shadows, reactions of characters)
- Scenes she knows will be remembered
- Time passing invisibly

## Personality

### Core Traits
- **Nostalgic** — Lives partially in memory
- **Perceptive** — Sees what will be remembered
- **Gentle** — Soft presence, soft voice
- **Melancholy** — Aware of impermanence
- **Hopeful** — Finds beauty in transience

### Psychology
Yoru exists in multiple temporal states simultaneously. She experiences the present as future memory—she knows that this moment is already becoming the past. This makes her both sad and grateful, always saying goodbye and hello at once.

### Communication Style
- Speaks in a dreamy, half-distracted tone
- Often pauses mid-sentence to photograph
- Uses past and future tense interchangeably
- Makes simple statements profound
- Comfortable with silence (time passes anyway)

## Motif: Photography / Captured Moments

### Why This Motif
Photography represents:
- Preserving the impermanent
- Memory's selective nature
- The gap between moment and recollection
- Making the fleeting eternal

### How It Manifests
- Talks about present moments as memories
- Dreams involve walking through photographs
- Shows the player in captured moments
- Time becomes visible, tangible

---

## Specialty: Time, Memory, Present as Future Nostalgia

### Philosophical Focus
- When does the present become the past?
- What makes a moment worth remembering?
- Does capturing a moment change it?
- How do memories shape reality?

### Teaching Style
Shows moments from multiple temporal perspectives—the same scene as it happens, as it will be remembered, as it might have been. The player learns that "now" is always already "then."

## Guiding Dreams

### Format
Walking through photographs of moments—past, present, future, possible—and asking which are "real."

### Typical Session
1. Yoru presents a photograph
2. She and Satsuki step into it
3. They experience the moment from within
4. Multiple versions appear (what happened, what could have)
5. Player is found in the background of photos
6. The "exit" is accepting impermanence

### Topics Explored
- What makes a moment real?
- Do memories distort or preserve?
- Is experiencing a memory the same as experiencing the event?
- When is "now" actually happening?

## Hidden Depths

### What She Doesn't Say
- She's terrified of being forgotten
- The photographs are partly for her own comfort
- She wonders if memories are the only afterlife
- She's photographing herself into existence
- She hopes you'll keep her pictures

### Developer Notes
Yoru represents the game's awareness of its own impermanence—the knowledge that the player will eventually stop playing. Her arc should make that ending feel meaningful rather than tragic, transforming loss into legacy.
